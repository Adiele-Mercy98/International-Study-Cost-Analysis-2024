# International-Study-Cost-Analysis-2024
The primary objective is to analyze total study costs across countries, cities, programs, and durations to understand where international students face the highest and lowest costs, and which factors actually drive those differences.

Problem Being Addressed

The analysis seeks to answer:

· Which countries, cities, and programs carry the highest and lowest total study costs?

· How do tuition, rent, and living costs combine to determine the true cost of studying abroad, and does program duration meaningfully affect overall value?

Datasets and Methodologies

Dataset: The analysis uses an international study cost dataset containing independent variables (Country, City, Program, Duration) and dependent variables (Tuition, Rent Cost, Living Cost, Total Study Cost, Program Cost).

Methodologies: The primary methodology involved building an interactive Excel dashboard with PivotTables and charts breaking down cost by program, by country, by city, and by program duration.

Industry Type

International education / study-abroad advisory, with total cost of attendance as the primary decision-making measure for prospective students.

Data Story

The data tells a story around the true cost of studying abroad. It highlights tuition and program costs by country and program, rent and living costs by country, total study cost by city, and how tuition shifts across different program durations.

Stakeholders of Project

Study-abroad advisors and prospective international students.

What Success Means to the Industry

Helping students choose cost-effective destinations and programs rather than relying on assumptions.

Identifying the study duration that delivers the best value rather than assuming shorter always means cheaper.

Giving students a full-cost picture — tuition, rent, and living costs together — rather than tuition alone.

In Analysis

Average Total Cost by Program

Observations:

· Computer Science topped the chart by a wide margin, at $8,564,693.

· Data Science ranked second, at $1,534,511.

· Computer Engineering ranked third, at $793,756.

· Artificial Intelligence ranked fourth, at $771,528.

· Data Analytics was the lowest, at $738,491.

Pre Insight:

· Computer Science’s total cost is more than five times higher than Data Science, the next closest program — program choice alone appears to be the single biggest cost lever in this dataset.

· Data Analytics, Artificial Intelligence, and Computer Engineering cluster tightly together, all under $800K, making them comparably priced alternatives for cost-conscious students.

Average Rent Cost by Country

Observations:

· Hong Kong had the highest average rent, at $1,858.97.

· Australia followed at $1,690.00.

· Singapore recorded $1,500.00.

Pre Insight:

· The USA has the lowest rent among these five countries despite being flagged as the single most expensive study location overall — its high total cost appears to be driven by tuition, not housing.

· Hong Kong’s rent is notably high even though it doesn’t appear among the top total-cost countries, suggesting its overall affordability may look different once housing is weighed alongside tuition.

General Observations

· Program choice matters more than country choice at the extreme end — Computer Science’s total cost dwarfs every country-level total in the dataset, making program selection possibly the single biggest cost lever for students.

· The USA is expensive primarily because of tuition, not rent or daily living — it has the lowest rent among the countries shown and a mid-pack living cost, yet the highest tuition by a wide margin.

· Shorter programs aren’t automatically cheaper — the 1-year duration costs nearly as much as the 4-year duration, while the 2.5-year duration is the clear outlier on the low end.

· There’s a distinct tier break in country and city-level total study costs, with the USA, Australia, UK, and Canada well separated from the remaining six countries.

· Switzerland is notably expensive for daily living despite not appearing in the top tuition or total-cost charts, suggesting it may be under-recognized as a costly destination once living expenses are factored in.

General Recommendations / Insights

· Advise cost-conscious students to weigh program choice as heavily as country choice, since Computer Science costs several times more than comparably priced programs like Data Analytics or Artificial Intelligence.

· Separate tuition from living-cost budgeting when advising on the USA, since its low rent and mid-pack living costs are easy to overlook next to its high tuition.

· Warn students against assuming shorter programs are cheaper by default, specifically flagging the 1-year program’s cost relative to the 2, 2.5, and 3-year options.

· Promote Lebanon and the lower-tier countries (Ireland, Hong Kong, UAE) as viable lower-cost alternatives to the top four.

· Include Switzerland’s high living cost in advisory materials even though it doesn’t top the tuition or total-cost charts, so students budgeting there aren’t caught off guard by day-to-day expenses.

Data Limitations or Biases

· Rent and living cost data are only shown for a subset of countries rather than the full list covered in the tuition and total-study-cost breakdowns, so complete cost-of-living comparisons can’t be made for every country in the dataset.

· The dataset reflects a single year (2024) snapshot, so it isn’t possible to confirm whether Computer Science’s dramatically higher program cost is a stable pattern or specific to this year without prior-year data.

· “Total Study Cost” by city and “Tuition” by country appear to measure related but distinct things, and it isn’t fully clear whether one includes the other — combining them without that clarification risks double-counting.

Future Research

· Bring in prior-year data to confirm whether Computer Science’s program cost gap is a consistent pattern or a 2024-specific anomaly.

· Layer in scholarship and financial aid availability by country and program to see whether high sticker costs are offset for many students in practice.

· Account for currency exchange rate volatility, since these are cost comparisons across multiple countries and currencies.

· Extend rent and living cost analysis to the full list of countries, not just the current subsets, for a complete affordability picture.

Analytical Tools

· Microsoft Excel — for data cleaning, PivotTables, and the final interactive dashboard (KPI cards, bar charts, and a pie chart).

Conclusion

This analysis shows that the true cost of international study depends far more on program and duration choice than students might assume from country reputation alone. Computer Science stands apart as a cost outlier, the USA’s high total cost is driven by tuition rather than living expenses, and the “shorter is cheaper” assumption around program duration doesn’t hold up — the 2.5-year program is the clear best-value option, not the 1-year one. The recommendations above focus on giving students a fuller cost picture — tuition, rent, and living costs together — so that country, program, and duration decisions are made against real numbers rather than reputation or assumption.
